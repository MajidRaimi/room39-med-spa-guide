# Google Business Profile for Med Spas: The 12-Point Checklist

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the page Google shows for most local med spa searches. It feeds Maps, the local pack, and the entity data behind AI answers. To get it right: claim it, verify it, set the **Medical spa** category, keep your name, address, phone, and hours identical everywhere, then work the 12 points below. Most clinics can finish the list in a week.

This checklist is written for an owner or practice manager, not a developer. Every point ends with a fix line you can hand to whoever manages the profile.

## Why this profile matters more than your website for local searches

Google does not rank a med spa locally the way it ranks a blog post. For local results it uses three factors: **relevance**, **distance**, and **prominence**. Google describes relevance as how well a profile matches the search, distance as how far the business is from the searcher, and prominence as how well known the business is ([Google Business Profile Help, "Tips to improve your local ranking on Google," accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)).

Your website influences relevance and prominence. Your profile is where Google reads the facts it uses for all three. Google states plainly that "businesses with complete and accurate info are more likely to show up in local search results," and that inaccurate info can keep a profile from appearing for relevant local searches ([Google Business Profile Help, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)).

The audience has moved, too. BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey found that use of ChatGPT and other generative AI tools for local recommendations rose from 6% to 45% year over year, making AI the third most popular source of business recommendations in its sample ([BrightLocal, published 2026-02-11, updated 2026-09-14](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)). Those systems draw on the same public facts your profile and site supply. When the facts disagree, confidence drops.

## The 12-point Google Business Profile checklist for med spas

Work the list in order. Points 1 through 4 are the foundation. If the profile is unverified or the name is wrong, the rest of the work will not hold.

### 1. Claim and verify the profile

Adding and claiming a profile is free. Google lets you claim an existing listing or create one, and after you claim it you control how the business information shows up ([Google Business Profile Help, "Add or claim your Business Profile," accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2911778)). Verification methods are chosen by Google and cannot be changed; depending on your business, you may need to verify more than one way, and video verification is often the fastest option ([Google Business Profile Help, "Verify your business on Google," accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242)).

Search your clinic name plus your city first. If a listing already exists, claim it rather than creating a duplicate. Google's guidelines are explicit that "there should only be one profile per business," because duplicates cause display problems across Maps and Search ([Google Business Profile Help, "Guidelines for representing your business on Google," accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)).

**Fix:** Confirm the profile status says verified. If it is unverified, complete verification this week. If you find a duplicate, request a merge instead of leaving both live.

### 2. Lock your name to your real-world name

Google wants your profile name to match "your business's real-world name, as used consistently on your storefront, website, stationery, and as known to customers" ([Google guidelines, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). That means the name on the front door, not the name you wish would rank.

Adding a city or treatment to the name is the most common med spa mistake here. "Glow Aesthetics" is correct. "Glow Aesthetics — Botox, Fillers & Laser Phoenix" can get the listing suspended and it does not help ranking the way owners hope. The city already lives in the address and the service area.

**Fix:** Set the name to the exact name on your signage and invoices. Move treatment words into your services and description instead.

### 3. Set the primary category to Medical spa

Google's guidance is to "choose the fewest number of categories it takes to describe your overall core business" ([Google guidelines, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). Categories are how Google matches a profile to a search, so the primary category carries the most weight.

For an independent medical aesthetics clinic, **Medical spa** is usually the right primary category. Add a small set of accurate secondaries, such as Skin care clinic, Medical clinic, or Wellness center. Do not stack categories you do not practise. A wrong category sends the wrong searches and weakens relevance.

**Fix:** Set the primary category, then audit secondaries. If you would not offer the service in that category on your price list, remove it.

### 4. Get the address pin or service area right

Google asks that your address and service area be "accurate and precise" ([Google guidelines, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). If patients visit, place the pin on your entrance, not the middle of the block or a nearby plaza. If you run a mobile practice, hide the address and set a service area instead of publishing a home address or a virtual office.

Distance is one of the three local ranking factors, so a wrong pin hurts exactly the searches you want. Never use a mailbox store or a virtual office as your address. It can trigger a suspension and it undermines the entity facts.

**Fix:** Open the pin in Maps, zoom to street level, and move it to the door. If you are mobile-only, switch to a service-area profile.

### 5. Publish complete and accurate hours

Hours are part of the completeness Google ties to local visibility ([Google Business Profile Help, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)). Add regular hours, then add special hours for holidays, conference weeks, and any planned closure. A profile that says "Open" while the door is locked earns bad reviews and hurts prominence.

For a med spa, list the hours patients can actually book and reach a person. If your phone rolls to voicemail after 5pm, set the closing time to when the phone is answered, not when the treatment room empties.

**Fix:** Set regular and special hours. Review them on the first Monday of each month.

### 6. Add your services with plain descriptions

Add each core treatment as a service, with a short description and a price or price range where you publish one. Keep the names identical to the names on your website. If your site says "Injectables" and your profile says "Wrinkle treatments," Google and patients have to guess whether they are the same thing.

The description field is for useful information about services and products, the business mission, and its history, and Google requires it to be honest and relevant ([Google guidelines, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). Describe the treatment in plain words. Do not promise clinical outcomes, and do not paste the same block of text into every service.

**Fix:** Add your top 10 treatments. For each, write two sentences: what it is and who books it.

### 7. Write a business description that names your treatments and your city

The description is a chance to state what you do in the words patients use. Name your core treatments, your city or metro, and the fact that you are an independent clinic. Keep it for humans. Google's field is not a place for keyword lists, and content with "no clear association" with the business is not allowed ([Google guidelines, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)).

**Fix:** Write 500 to 750 characters. Include your top three treatments and your city once each.

### 8. Add real, business-specific photos

Google publishes specific photo guidance: add at least three exterior photos, at least three interior photos, and at least three photos that show your team providing services ([Google Business Profile Help, "Tips for business-specific photos on your Business Profile," accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6123536)). Cover photos and logos help recognition, but the business-specific photos carry the weight.

For a med spa, exterior photos should be shot from each direction a patient approaches. Interior photos should show the treatment rooms and the waiting area as they really look. Service photos should show your providers at work, with patient consent. Stock photos and borrowed images do not represent your business and can be removed.

**Fix:** Book a one-hour photo session. Upload at least nine photos and add new ones monthly.

### 9. Turn on messaging and add your booking link

Add your booking URL so patients can move from the profile to your calendar in one tap. Add messaging only if you can answer within a day. Google's local ranking page notes that responding to customer interactions shows you value feedback ([Google Business Profile Help, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)). An unanswered message is worse than no messaging button.

**Fix:** Add the booking link. Turn messaging on only with a named person who owns replies.

### 10. Monitor and answer Q&A

Google Business Profile Q&A lets anyone ask a question and anyone answer it. Left alone, your profile can fill with old questions and wrong answers from strangers. Seed the questions you already get on the phone, then answer each one as the owner.

**Fix:** Post the five questions your front desk hears most, with short answers. Check the Q&A section weekly and flag anything that breaks Google's content rules.

### 11. Build a review flow and reply to every review

Reviews are a prominence signal, and Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking ([Google Business Profile Help, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)). They also move patients before they ever call. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found that 97% of consumers still use reviews to guide purchase decisions, and 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews ([BrightLocal, 2026-02-11](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)).

Replies matter as much as collection. The same survey found 89% of consumers expect a response to their review, and 42% say they are unlikely to use a business that never replies ([BrightLocal, 2026-02-11](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)).

One rule is not negotiable. Google prohibits offering incentives such as payment, discounts, or free goods and services in exchange for reviews, or for revising or removing a negative one ([Google Business Profile Help, "Prohibited & restricted content," accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2622994)). Ask every patient, gate nothing, and never buy reviews.

**Fix:** Add a review request to your checkout or follow-up text. Reply to every review within two business days.

### 12. Add attributes and keep the profile active

Attributes tell Google and patients what to expect: wheelchair-accessible entrance, wheelchair-accessible restroom, LGBTQ+ friendly, and similar. Fill in the ones that are true. Then keep the profile active with posts or updates at least monthly. Prominence rewards profiles that look alive.

**Fix:** Complete every applicable attribute. Schedule one update a month with a treatment, an offer, or a team note.

## Which three points to fix first

If you only have one afternoon, fix the points that block everything else.

1. **Verification (point 1).** An unverified profile competes at a disadvantage and cannot be managed properly.
2. **Name and category (points 2 and 3).** These set relevance. Every other improvement is measured against them.
3. **Hours and address (points 4 and 5).** These are the facts patients and AI assistants repeat. Wrong facts create wrong answers.

Reviews come next, because they compound. Photos and posts are the easiest to maintain once the foundation is right.

## Med spa mistakes that can get your profile suspended

These are drawn from Google's guidelines and prohibited-content rules, and they are common in aesthetics.

- **A keyword-stuffed name.** City and treatment words in the name.
- **More than one profile for one location.** Duplicates split your signals and confuse Maps.
- **A virtual office or mailbox address.** Use a real, staffed location or a service area.
- **Incentivized reviews.** Discounts and gifts in exchange for reviews break Google's rules.
- **Stock or borrowed photos.** Business-specific photos are required to represent the business.
- **Misleading services or claims.** No invented treatments and no clinical promises.

**Fix:** Run the list above against your own profile once a quarter.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a med spa?

Yes. You can add or claim your profile at no charge, and once claimed you control how the information appears in Search and Maps ([Google Business Profile Help, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2911778)). You may pay a vendor to manage it, but the profile itself is free.

### What category should a med spa use on Google?

Use **Medical spa** as the primary category for a typical independent aesthetics clinic, then add only the fewest accurate secondary categories ([Google guidelines, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). Remove any category for a service you do not offer.

### Can I put keywords in my Google Business Profile name?

No. Google requires the name to reflect your real-world business name as used on your storefront and website ([Google guidelines, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)). Adding a city or treatments to the name risks suspension and does not replace proper categories and services.

### How many photos should a med spa add to Google?

Google's guidance is at least three exterior photos, at least three interior photos, and at least three service photos ([Google Business Profile Help, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6123536)). Add new, real photos monthly so the profile stays current.

### How do I get more Google reviews for my med spa?

Ask every patient, at the moment they are happiest, and make leaving a review a single tap. Do not gate, filter, or incentivize reviews. Google prohibits incentives for reviews, and the 2026 BrightLocal survey shows that simply asking works: 83% of consumers who were asked to leave a review went on to leave one ([BrightLocal, 2026-02-11](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)).

### Do I need a separate Google Business Profile for each location?

You need one profile per physical location, and you should not run two profiles for the same location. Google says there should only be one profile per business because duplicates cause problems across Maps and Search ([Google guidelines, accessed 2026-10-19](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)).

### What if my med spa's Google Business Profile is suspended?

Find the rule you broke, fix the root cause, and appeal. The usual causes are a keyword-stuffed name, a virtual address, a duplicate listing, or incentivized reviews. Correct the profile before you appeal so the reviewer sees the fix rather than a promise.
